## B. Amazon Review Mining

**Source:** Amazon.de men's cotton socks category + DeadSoxy editorial (100% cotton socks guide) + Ask Andy forum + customer review aggregates

### Verbatim customer language — the problem

**On bunching / migration:**
- "that awkward and uncomfortable feeling of a crease under your toes or your heels when your socks slouch"
- "I had to stop what I was doing and fix it in the moment rather than bear the feeling of scrunched up socks"
- "socks that bunch, slip, or fall"
- Socks described as forcing the wearer to become a "heel slipper" — constantly pulling socks back up

**On holes / durability:**
- "a pack of Gold Toe socks developed real, serious holes in more than half of the socks within 3 months"
- "Dockers mercerized cotton socks pilled after 1 washing and basically fell apart after about 10 wearings"
- Multiple reviewers: holes appearing at heel and toe within 2–3 months
- "Loch nach wenigen Wochen" (hole after just a few weeks) — common German-language complaint

**On the repeat-buying trap:**
- "I keep buying new packs and the same thing happens"
- Customers buying replacement packs every 3–4 months, unaware that the material is the cause
- Belief that "it's just how socks are" — normalized failure

**On wash behavior:**
- Socks that "became very tight after washing at 30 degrees"
- Size 43–46 running too short and too tight after first wash
- Cold-water-only instructions seen as an inconvenience, not a red flag

**On moisture / foot comfort:**
- Cotton described as "breathable for the first hour" but creating foot dampness by mid-afternoon
- Bacteria/odor complaints by end of day in closed shoes

### What customers praise when they find a better sock:
- "Remains excellent after a year" (mat & Vic's testimonial)
- Socks that don't require adjusting during the day
- Socks that look the same after 50 washes
- "Finally socks that don't ruin my day"

## C. Avatar Segmentation

**Primary avatar (most in active tension):**

**Who:** European professional male, 28–50. Works in an office, wears dress shoes or clean sneakers. Buys socks in multi-packs. Considers himself pragmatic about clothing — pays attention to shirts and shoes, thinks socks are a commodity.

**Current belief about the problem:**
He doesn't identify as someone with a sock problem. He notices the bunching, the hole at the heel after two months, the sweaty feeling by 3pm — but explains it away: "these are just cheap socks," "I should buy a better brand," "it's probably my shoes." The villain is invisible to him because 100% cotton is the default.

**The moment of tension:**
He's in a meeting or at a work event. By early afternoon, one sock has migrated down into his shoe. He shifts his foot. He can feel it bunching under his arch. He can't fix it without drawing attention. He finishes the meeting uncomfortable. That night he throws the socks in the wash and doesn't think about it again. He replaces the pack in three months.

**Language they use (verbatim or near-verbatim):**
- "My socks bunch up by the afternoon"
- "I keep buying new packs and it always happens again"
- "I figured it was just socks — you know, they all fall apart"
- "By end of day my feet feel damp"
- "There's always a hole at the heel after a couple months"

**What has to happen for them to buy:**
1. They need to see that the failure is the material's fault — not theirs, not bad luck
2. They need to understand that the alternative exists and is specific (a blend formula, not just "better quality")
3. They need a mechanism — something that explains WHY the blend solves it (the 78/20 Formula)
4. They need it to feel discovered, not sold to
