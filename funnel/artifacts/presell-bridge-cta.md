## Five Things to Check Before You Buy a Sock

The market is full of socks labeled "premium" and "long-lasting."

Most of them are still 100% cotton.

Before buying any sock, check these five things.

---

**Thing to check #1: Is the cotton combed or standard?**

The label says "100% cotton" either way.

Combed cotton has the short, loose fibers removed before knitting — the fibers that work free and roughen the surface after 15–20 washes.

Standard cotton includes them.

If the label doesn't specify "combed cotton" or "gekämmte Baumwolle," assume it's standard.

> **The threshold:** Look for "combed cotton" or "combed" on the label or product description. If it's absent, the surface will change within 3 months of regular wear.

---

**Thing to check #2: Is there a polyamide percentage?**

Polyamide (sometimes listed as nylon) is the fiber that reinforces high-friction zones.

Without it, the heel and toe are made from the same material as the ankle — which means they fail first.

> **The threshold:** Look for 15–25% polyamide in the blend. Below 15%, the reinforcement effect is minimal. Above 25%, the sock begins to feel synthetic rather than comfortable.

---

**Thing to check #3: Is there elastane in the blend?**

Elastane provides elastic recovery — the ability to spring back after stretching.

Without it, the sock migrates.

A small percentage goes a long way.

> **The threshold:** Even 2% elastane changes the behavior of the sock. Look for any elastane percentage on the label. If it's absent, the sock will eventually migrate down the foot.

---

**Thing to check #4: What are the care instructions?**

A sock that requires cold wash only is a sock that was built under material tension.

A well-blended sock can handle a normal laundry cycle.

> **The threshold:** The sock should tolerate at least 40°C. Cold-wash-only instructions are a warning sign — not a sign of quality care.

---

**Thing to check #5: Is the construction different at the heel and toe?**

Uniform construction across the entire sock means the high-wear zones get the same protection as the ankle.

They don't need the same protection.

They need more.

> **The threshold:** Look for language about "reinforced heel and toe" or "zone reinforcement." If the product description doesn't mention it, assume uniform construction.
