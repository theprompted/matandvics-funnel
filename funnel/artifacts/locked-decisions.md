## Villain
**100% cotton** — the material itself is the problem. Not cheap manufacturing or bad brands — the fiber architecture of pure cotton is inherently mismatched to what feet need.

## Coined Term
**The 78/20 Formula** — 78% combed cotton / 20% polyamide / 2% elastane. Sounds proprietary. Implies engineering, not compromise.

## Avatar (problem in their own words)
European professional male, 25–50. Practical, responds to evidence. "My socks bunch up at the arch by early afternoon. I keep buying new packs and the same thing happens — hole at the heel in a few months. I figured it was just socks."

## Presell Headline
**5 Reasons 100% Cotton Socks Are Designed to Fail You**

## 5 Reasons (villain's specific failure modes)

1. **Elastic memory** — Cotton stretches and doesn't return. No structural anchor means the sock migrates down the foot by afternoon. It's not a sizing issue — it's the fiber.

2. **Moisture retention** — Cotton absorbs sweat and holds it against the skin. "Breathable" for the first hour; by hour four in a closed shoe, the foot is sitting in what it produced in the morning. Wicking requires synthetic fiber.

3. **Uniform construction on a non-uniform foot** — Heel and toe absorb ~8,000 friction events per day. Cotton socks put the same material everywhere. The high-stress zones arrive at failure first while the rest of the sock looks fine.

4. **Wash sensitivity** — Cotton shrinks in hot water. Cold-water-only instructions are an admission of material limitation. A sock that can't go in with the jeans is a sock that requires behavior change to survive.

5. **Fiber degradation over wash cycles** — Uncombed cotton short fibers work loose and pill. Surface roughens by wash 15–20. The sock that felt right on day one is a different object by month three. "Combed cotton" removes this — but most cotton socks don't say whether they are.
